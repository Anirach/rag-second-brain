# Medical Informatics: Introduction and Overview

## What is Medical Informatics?

Medical Informatics (also known as Health Informatics or Biomedical Informatics) is the interdisciplinary field that studies and pursues the effective use of data, information, and knowledge for scientific inquiry, problem-solving, and decision-making in healthcare.

### Key Definition
> "The science of how to use data, information, and knowledge to improve human health and the delivery of healthcare services." — AMIA (American Medical Informatics Association)

---

## Core Components

### 1. Data
- Raw facts and figures (e.g., blood pressure reading: 120/80 mmHg)
- Electronic Health Records (EHR)
- Medical imaging files
- Laboratory results

### 2. Information
- Data that has been processed and organized
- Patient history summaries
- Diagnostic reports
- Treatment protocols

### 3. Knowledge
- Information that has been analyzed and understood
- Clinical guidelines
- Evidence-based medicine
- Decision support rules

---

## Key Areas of Medical Informatics

| Area | Description | Examples |
|------|-------------|----------|
| **Clinical Informatics** | Application of informatics at the point of care | EHR systems, CPOE, Clinical Decision Support |
| **Bioinformatics** | Computational analysis of biological data | Genomics, proteomics, drug discovery |
| **Public Health Informatics** | Population-level health data management | Disease surveillance, epidemiology systems |
| **Consumer Health Informatics** | Patient-facing health technology | Patient portals, health apps, wearables |
| **Imaging Informatics** | Medical image management and analysis | PACS, radiology AI, image processing |

---

## Essential Technologies

### Electronic Health Records (EHR)
- Digital version of patient charts
- Enables real-time, secure access to patient information
- Supports interoperability between healthcare providers

### Health Information Exchange (HIE)
- Secure sharing of health data across organizations
- Types: Directed exchange, Query-based, Consumer-mediated

### Clinical Decision Support Systems (CDSS)
- Provides clinicians with knowledge and patient-specific information
- Drug interaction alerts
- Diagnostic suggestions
- Treatment recommendations

### Telemedicine & Telehealth
- Remote delivery of healthcare services
- Video consultations
- Remote patient monitoring
- Mobile health (mHealth)

## Data Standards in Healthcare

### Terminology Standards
- **ICD-10/ICD-11** — International Classification of Diseases
- **SNOMED CT** — Systematized Nomenclature of Medicine
- **LOINC** — Logical Observation Identifiers Names and Codes
- **RxNorm** — Normalized names for clinical drugs

### Messaging Standards
- **HL7 v2/v3** — Health Level Seven messaging
- **FHIR** — Fast Healthcare Interoperability Resources
- **DICOM** — Digital Imaging and Communications in Medicine
- **CDA** — Clinical Document Architecture

### Technical Challenges

- Alert fatigue

## Summary

Medical Informatics is a rapidly evolving field that sits at the intersection of:
- **Healthcare** — Clinical knowledge and patient care
- **Information Science** — Data management and analysis
- **Computer Science** — Technology and systems
- **Social Science** — Human behavior and organizational change

The ultimate goal is to improve healthcare delivery, patient outcomes, and population health through the intelligent use of data and technology.
