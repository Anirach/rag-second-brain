Introduction

What is ClawdBot?

ClawdBot is an AI-powered personal assistant that combines the reasoning capabilities of Claude with practical tools for file management, web searching, document generation, and automated scheduling. Unlike simple chatbots, ClawdBot can execute real actions: search the web, fetch and analyze documents, create files, set up automated reminders, and maintain context across sessions.

For academics, ClawdBot serves as a tireless research partner that can help you discover literature, organize your findings, draft documents, manage citations, improve your writing, and keep your research projects on track-all through natural conversation.

Why Use AI for Academic Research?

⏱️ Time Efficiency
Automate repetitive tasks like citation formatting and literature searching
📚 Comprehensive Coverage
Search across multiple sources simultaneously for thorough reviews
✍️ Writing Quality
Get instant feedback on clarity, tone, and academic conventions
📋 Organization
Maintain structured notes and never lose track of sources
⚠️ Academic Integrity Notice

ClawdBot is a tool to assist your research, not replace your intellectual contribution. Always verify AI-generated content, properly cite all sources, and ensure your work reflects your own analysis and insights. Check your institution's policies on AI assistance in academic work.

USE CASE 1

Literature Review Assistant

Use Case 1: Literature Review Assistant

Overview

The Literature Review Assistant helps you discover, retrieve, and organize academic papers on any topic. Using web search capabilities optimized for academic content, ClawdBot can find recent publications, fetch their content, summarize key findings, and organize everything into structured notes for your review.

Why It's Valuable for Academics

  • Efficient Discovery: Search multiple academic databases and sources in seconds
  • Smart Summarization: Get concise summaries of papers without reading each one fully
  • Structured Organization: Maintain organized notes with consistent formatting
  • Gap Identification: Easily identify research gaps and opportunities
  • Time Savings: Reduce initial literature scanning from days to hours

📋 Prerequisites

  • ClawdBot installed and configured
  • Web search capability enabled (Brave Search API)
  • A workspace folder for storing literature notes
  • Clear research questions or topics defined

Step-by-Step Setup

1

Define Your Research Scope

Before starting, clearly define your research questions, key terms, and the scope of your review (time period, disciplines, geographic focus if relevant).

2

Create a Literature Notes Structure

Set up a consistent folder structure for your findings:

research/
├── literature/
│   ├── notes/
│   │   ├── 2024-01-15_transformers_nlp.md
│   │   └── 2024-01-16_attention_mechanisms.md
│   ├── summaries/
│   └── bibliography.md
└── queries.md
3

Craft Effective Search Queries

Use academic-specific search terms. Adding "site:arxiv.org" or "site:scholar.google.com" or "filetype:pdf" can help focus results on academic content.

Pro Tips

💡 Pro Tip: Use Boolean Search Operators

Combine terms effectively: "transformer AND (efficiency OR optimization) AND NLP -vision" helps exclude irrelevant computer vision papers when searching for NLP work.

💡 Pro Tip: Search by Author

Once you identify key researchers in your area, ask ClawdBot to find recent papers by specific authors: "Find papers by Yoshua Bengio on attention mechanisms from 2023"

💡 Pro Tip: Create Weekly Review Sessions

Set up a recurring search for new papers in your area. Ask ClawdBot to search for papers from the past week on your topic every Monday morning.

Common Pitfalls to Avoid

⚠️ Pitfall: Over-relying on Summaries

AI summaries are starting points, not substitutes for reading. Always read the full methodology and results sections of papers you plan to cite heavily.

⚠️ Pitfall: Not Verifying Sources

Always verify that papers exist and that the citation information is correct. Check the actual publication venue and author list.

⚠️ Pitfall: Missing Preprints vs Published

ArXiv papers may differ from their final published versions. Note whether you're referencing a preprint or the peer-reviewed publication.

USE CASE 2

Research Document Generation

Use Case 2: Research Document Generation

Overview

Research Document Generation enables you to create well-structured academic documents including literature reviews, research proposals, methodology sections, and reports. ClawdBot can generate properly formatted documents with appropriate sections, citations, and academic conventions.

Why It's Valuable for Academics

  • Structural Consistency: Ensure all documents follow proper academic structure
  • Template Efficiency: Generate standard sections (abstracts, methodology) quickly
  • Format Flexibility: Output to Markdown, HTML, or prepare for conversion to DOCX/PDF
  • Integration: Pull from your literature notes to create cohesive review documents
  • Draft Generation: Create first drafts that you can refine and personalize

📋 Prerequisites

  • ClawdBot with file write capabilities
  • Pandoc installed (for DOCX/PDF conversion): apt install pandoc
  • LaTeX distribution for PDF generation (optional): apt install texlive
  • Your institution's formatting guidelines

Step-by-Step Setup

1

Prepare Your Templates

Create template files for common document types. Store these in a templates folder:

templates/
├── literature_review.md
├── research_proposal.md
├── methodology.md
└── thesis_chapter.md
2

Define Document Requirements

When requesting a document, specify: word count, required sections, citation style, and any institutional requirements.

3

Set Up Conversion Pipeline

For DOCX/PDF output, ensure Pandoc is configured:

# Convert Markdown to DOCX
pandoc document.md -o document.docx --reference-doc=template.docx

# Convert Markdown to PDF
pandoc document.md -o document.pdf --pdf-engine=xelatex

Different disciplines have different conventions. A humanities literature review looks different from a STEM one. Specify your field explicitly.

Supported Citation Styles

Style Common Use Key Features
APA 7th Edition Psychology, Social Sciences, Education Author-date in-text; DOI included
MLA 9th Edition Humanities, Literature, Languages Author-page in-text; containers
Chicago (Notes) History, Arts, Humanities Footnotes/endnotes; bibliography

USE CASE 4

Writing Assistant & Proofreader

Use Case 4: Writing Assistant & Proofreader

Overview

The Writing Assistant helps you improve your academic writing through grammar and style checking, clarity improvements, paraphrasing, and maintaining appropriate academic tone. Whether you're polishing an abstract, refining a methodology section, or ensuring your thesis reads professionally, ClawdBot provides constructive feedback while preserving your voice.

Why It's Valuable for Academics

  • Style Consistency: Maintain formal academic tone throughout long documents
  • Clarity Improvements: Identify and fix unclear or convoluted sentences
  • Grammar Accuracy: Catch errors that spell-checkers miss
  • Non-Native Support: Get help with English academic conventions
  • Instant Feedback: Get suggestions without waiting for peer reviews

📋 Prerequisites

  • ClawdBot running (no special configuration needed)
  • Your draft text ready for review
  • Understanding of your target journal/venue style preferences

Step-by-Step Workflow

1

Prepare Your Text

Have your draft ready. You can paste text directly or ask ClawdBot to read from a file.

2

Specify Your Goals

Tell ClawdBot what kind of feedback you want: grammar only, style improvements, clarity, conciseness, or comprehensive review.

3

Provide Context

Mention your field, target audience, and any specific style guides (e.g., "This is for a Nature submission").

4

Review and Accept/Modify Suggestions

Go through suggestions critically-you know your content best.

Real Examples

Example 1: Abstract Improvement
👤 User
Please review and improve this abstract for clarity and academic tone:

"We made a new system that uses AI to help with finding bugs in code. It works really good and is better than other tools. We tested it on many projects and developers said they liked it. The results show it can find more bugs than humans can in less time."

Here's a revised version with improved academic tone and clarity:

Revised Abstract:
"We present a novel AI-assisted system for automated bug detection in software code. A user study with 28 professional developers revealed high acceptance rates (μ = 4.2/5.0) and indicated strong potential for integration into existing development workflows."

Changes Made:

Original Revised Reason
"made a new system" "present a novel system" Academic verb choice
"works really good" "demonstrates significant improvements" Grammar + specificity
"tested it on many projects" "empirical evaluation across 15 projects" Quantified claim
"developers said they liked it" "high acceptance rates (μ = 4.2/5.0)" Quantified evidence

Example 2: Conciseness Editing
👤 User
This paragraph is too long. Help me cut it by 40% while keeping the key information:

"In order to be able to investigate the effects that climate change has been having on agricultural productivity in the region, we collected data from a total of 47 different farms that were located in various different parts of the study area. The data that we collected included information about crop yields, which we measured in metric tons per hectare, as well as information about temperature and precipitation levels that were recorded at each individual farm location over the period of time spanning from 2010 to 2023."
🤖 ClawdBot
Revised (40% shorter):

"To investigate climate change effects on regional agricultural productivity, we collected data from 47 farms across the study area. Data included crop yields (metric tons/hectare) and local temperature and precipitation records from 2010-2023."

Word count:
• Original: 97 words
• Revised: 41 words (58% reduction)

Cuts made:
• "In order to be able to" → "To"
• "a total of 47 different farms that were located in various different parts" → "47 farms across"
• "The data that we collected included information about" → "Data included"
• "which we measured in" → parenthetical notation
• "over the period of time spanning from" → date range format

The key information-47 farms, crop yields, temperature, precipitation, 2010-2023 timeframe-is all preserved.
Example 3: Grammar and Style Check
👤 User
Check this methodology paragraph for grammar and style issues. I'm a non-native English speaker:

"The participants was recruited from the university student population. Each participant have completed a pre-screening questionnaire to ensure they meeting our inclusion criterias. The datas was collected over the course of 3 weeks period. We have analyzed the data using SPSS software."
🤖 ClawdBot
Corrected Version:

"Participants were recruited from the university student population. Each participant completed a pre-screening questionnaire to ensure they met our inclusion criteria. Data were collected over a 3-week period. We analyzed the data using SPSS software."

Grammar Corrections:

1. "participants was" → "Participants were"
   Plural subjects require plural verbs

2. "have completed" → "completed"
   Simple past for completed actions in methodology

3. "they meeting" → "they met"
   Past tense required after "ensure"

4. "criterias" → "criteria"
   "Criteria" is already plural (singular: "criterion")

5. "datas" → "Data"
   "Data" is already plural (or treated as mass noun)

6. "the course of 3 weeks period" → "a 3-week period"
   More concise phrasing

7. "We have analyzed" → "We analyzed"
   Simple past is standard for methodology sections

Style Note: Your journal may prefer "Data were collected" (treating data as plural) or "Data was collected" (mass noun). Check their style guide.

Pro Tips

💡 Pro Tip: Specify Your Level of Edit

Be specific about what you want:
• "Light edit-grammar only"
• "Medium edit-grammar and clarity"
• "Heavy edit-comprehensive rewrite for style"

💡 Pro Tip: Request Track Changes Format

Ask: "Show me changes in track-changes style" to see exactly what was modified. This helps you learn patterns in your writing.

💡 Pro Tip: Use for Translation Polishing

If you write first in your native language and translate, ask ClawdBot to "polish this translation for native English academic style."

Common Pitfalls to Avoid

⚠️ Pitfall: Losing Your Voice

Don't accept all suggestions blindly. Some "improvements" may remove field-specific terminology or your intentional stylistic choices.

⚠️ Pitfall: Over-Polishing

Academic writing should be clear but not artificially "perfect." Some personality in writing is acceptable and even valued.

⚠️ Pitfall: Not Checking Technical Terms

AI may "correct" field-specific terminology it doesn't recognize. Always verify technical terms remain accurate.

Pro Tips

💡 Pro Tip: Use Incremental Deadlines

Set intermediate deadlines for each section of a paper, not just the final submission. "Introduction by Monday, Methods by Wednesday" creates consistent progress.

💡 Pro Tip: Buffer Time

Set your personal deadlines 2-3 days before actual deadlines. This accounts for unexpected issues and reduces last-minute stress.

💡 Pro Tip: Weekly Reviews are Essential

The Monday morning review is the most valuable single habit. It prevents entire weeks from slipping away unproductively.

💡 Pro Tip: Log Blockers Immediately

When you hit a research blocker, log it right away. This creates a record for troubleshooting and helps identify patterns.

Common Pitfalls to Avoid

⚠️ Pitfall: Over-Scheduling

Don't create so many reminders that you start ignoring them. Quality over quantity-a few well-timed prompts beat constant notifications.

⚠️ Pitfall: Unrealistic Timelines

Be honest about how long tasks take. If writing 1,000 words takes you a full day, don't schedule 5,000 words per week.

⚠️ Pitfall: Not Adjusting

Plans change. Review and adjust your milestones monthly. A rigid plan that doesn't reflect reality is useless.

APPENDIX B

Troubleshooting

Common Issues and Solutions

Web Search Issues

❌ "Search returned no relevant academic results"

Solution: Add academic qualifiers to your search: "site:arxiv.org", "site:scholar.google.com", or include terms like "journal", "proceedings", or "et al."

❌ "Can't access paper content (paywall)"

Solution: Look for preprint versions on arXiv, search for author's personal website, or check if your institution provides access. Share the PDF directly if you have access.

Document Generation Issues

❌ "Generated document has incorrect formatting"

Solution: Be specific about formatting requirements. Provide a sample or template. Specify margins, fonts, and spacing explicitly.

❌ "Pandoc conversion failed"

Solution: Check that Pandoc is installed (pandoc --version). For PDF output, ensure LaTeX is installed (apt install texlive-xetex).

Citation Issues

❌ "Citation information is incomplete or incorrect"

Solution: Always verify citations against the original source. Provide DOIs when available for the most accurate metadata.

Reminder/Cron Issues

❌ "Reminder didn't trigger"

Solution: Check that ClawdBot is running. Verify the cron expression is correct. Ensure timezone settings match your expectations.

Getting Help
