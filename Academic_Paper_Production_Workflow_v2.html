1. Executive Summary

This workflow replaces the ad-hoc process that produced 23 versions over 3 weeks. Based on lessons from the MKG-RAG Second Brain paper journey, it targets 3 drafts in 7 days with higher novelty and fewer iterations.

Key improvements: experiments-first approach, upfront novelty validation, venue-first planning, batched reviews, and quality gates from day 1.

2. Problems with the Old Workflow

2.1 Experiments Came Too Late

v1-v9 built on proxy/synthetic claims. Real experiments (DDXPlus) didn't arrive until v15-v17. Papers without real results get destroyed in review.

2.2 No Novelty Validation Upfront

"Second Brain" was marketing until v23 when grounded in Extended Mind theory. Reviewers see through ungrounded framing immediately.

2.3 Quality System Was Reactive

QUALITY_LOCKS and 3-agent review gate created AFTER the v9 crisis (score dropped from 8.5 to 4.4). Should have been there from the start.

2.4 Too Many Small Iterations

23 versions, most were incremental patches. Each review-fix cycle costs time and introduces regression risk.

2.5 Venue Selected Late

Started targeting Information Fusion, pivoted to AIiH. Format conversion (IEEE to LNCS) wasted multiple versions.

2.6 Theoretical Grounding Added Last

Clark & Chalmers Extended Mind, Hutchins Distributed Cognition added in final versions. Should anchor novelty claims from the beginning.

3. The New Workflow: 3 Phases, 7 Days

3.1 Phase 1: Foundation (Day 1-2)

Tasks:

  • Pick target venue (format, page limit, scope, deadline)
  • Literature gap analysis: define novel contribution in ONE sentence
  • Run "Does this exist?" check (Semantic Scholar, Google Scholar, arXiv)
  • Build baseline experiments with real data
  • Design ablation-ready architecture (each component removable)
  • Create comparison table: what existing methods do vs what yours adds
  • Anchor method in existing theory (not just a name — cite cognitive science, information theory, etc.)

Deliverables table:

Deliverable Description Owner
Venue Brief Target venue, format, page limit, scope Journal Scout
Novelty Statement One sentence, grounded in theory Paper Architect
Literature Gap Table Existing methods vs yours Literature Lead
Baseline Results Real experiments, real data Data Analyst
Ablation Design Component removal plan Methodology Expert

3.2 Phase 2: Full Draft (Day 3-5)

Tasks:

  • Write complete paper with real results (not placeholders)
  • All sections: intro, related work, method, experiments, discussion, conclusion
  • Include all tables, figures, equations
  • Apply venue format from the start (e.g., llncs.cls, IEEE template)
  • 3-agent parallel review (peer-reviewer, methodology-expert, technical-writer)
  • Batch ALL fixes into single revision (Draft 2)

Review gate table:

Reviewer Focus Pass Criteria
Peer Reviewer Baselines, novelty, claims vs evidence No unsupported claims
Methodology Expert Statistical rigor, reproducibility Proper CIs, tests, caveats
Technical Writer Clarity, flow, readability Score ≥ 4.0/5.0

Rule: ALL THREE must pass. No exceptions. No "ship it with one approval."

3.3 Phase 3: Polish & Submit (Day 6-7)

Tasks:

  • Apply all Draft 2 review fixes
  • Final 3-agent review (should be minor issues only)
  • Format compliance check (page count, references, anonymization)
  • Generate supplementary materials if needed
  • Submit

4. Quality Gates (Active from Day 1)

4.1 QUALITY_LOCKS

Lock any element praised by reviewers. Never regress.

4.2 POSITIVE_FEEDBACK tracker

Cumulative praise log across review rounds.

4.3 Three-Agent Review Gate

Every draft reviewed by 3 agents in parallel. Unanimous approval required.

4.4 Novelty Checkpoint

Before Phase 2, verify the one-sentence novelty claim still holds after literature review.

4.5 Claims-Evidence Alignment

Every claim in the paper must map to a specific experiment, table, or citation. No orphan claims.

5. Consensus Checkpoints

The team operates on a "consult before act" principle. No agent proceeds to the next phase or major decision without consulting relevant team members first.

5.1 Phase 1: Novelty Summit (Day 1)

Before any writing begins, Literature Lead + Journal Scout + Paper Architect hold a Novelty Summit:

  • Literature Lead presents gap analysis findings
  • Journal Scout presents venue fit assessment
  • Paper Architect proposes the novelty statement
  • ALL THREE must agree the gap is real and the venue is right
  • If disagreement: iterate until consensus or escalate to human

5.2 Phase 2: Cross-Consultation Gates

Build consultation into the drafting process, not just at the end:

Gate Before Consultants Must Agree On
Experiment Design Running experiments Methodology Expert + Data Analyst + Peer Reviewer Variables, baselines, metrics are sound
Claims Check Writing discussion Peer Reviewer + Paper Architect + Methodology Expert Every claim maps to evidence
Ethics Screen Finalizing method section Ethics Reviewer + Methodology Expert No bias, proper consent, reproducibility
Writing Quality Completing each section Technical Writer + Paper Architect Clarity, flow, no unsupported claims

Rule: If ANY consultant raises a blocking concern, work stops until resolved. No "I'll fix it later."

5.3 Phase 3: Consensus Review

Instead of 3 independent reviews:

  1. All 3 reviewers (Peer Reviewer, Methodology Expert, Technical Writer) submit independent reviews
  2. Paper Architect compiles all feedback into a single conflict-resolution document
  3. Where reviewers disagree, they discuss and reach consensus
  4. ONE unified revision plan is created (not 3 separate fix lists)
  5. Fixes applied once, reviewed once → submit

5.4 Escalation Protocol

  • Minor disagreement (formatting, wording): Paper Architect decides
  • Major disagreement (methodology, claims, novelty): Human (Anirach) consulted
  • Deadlock (2+ agents can't agree after discussion): Human makes final call

This prevents the old pattern where one agent's fix broke another agent's approved work.

6. Novelty Boosters

6.1 Ablation-First Design

Build experiments so each component can be independently removed. Creates "unique contribution per component" — reviewers always ask for this.

6.2 Theoretical Grounding Early

Don't just name your method — anchor it in established theory from draft 1. Examples: Extended Mind (Clark & Chalmers), Distributed Cognition (Hutchins), Information Foraging Theory.

6.3 Comparison Table in Outline

Before writing, create a table of existing methods and what yours uniquely adds. If no clear gap, rethink the contribution.

6.4 Real-World Framing

Position contribution as solving a real problem, not just improving metrics. "Why does this matter to a doctor/engineer/user?"

6.5 Honest Limitations

Proactively state what your method does NOT do. Builds credibility and prevents reviewer attacks.

8. Checklist: Before Submitting

Numbered checklist:

  1. Novelty claim is one sentence, grounded in theory
  2. All experiments use real data (no proxies)
  3. Ablation study shows each component's unique contribution
  4. Statistical tests applied (Wilson CIs, McNemar's, etc.)
  5. External baselines included (not just your own variants)
  6. All 3 reviewers gave ACCEPT
  7. Page count within venue limit
  8. Double-blind compliance (no author names, no self-citations revealing identity)
  9. References complete and correctly formatted
  10. Supplementary materials prepared (code repo, appendix)

10. Lessons from the MKG-RAG Journey

Bullet points:

  • Honesty > Impressive Claims: Conceptual papers can be publishable without fake results
  • Quality Locks Prevent Regression: 23 locks ensured improvements were cumulative
  • Three Parallel Reviewers catch different issues: baselines, statistics, readability
  • ML Baselines Beating RAG Is Okay: Frame as accuracy vs generalization trade-off
  • Medical Datasets > General Benchmarks: Domain-specific data makes contribution tangible
  • Verify Numbers Before Locking: Wrong numbers hurt more than vague ones
  • Venue Selection Matters: Focused venue (AIiH) better than broad (Information Fusion)
  • "Second Brain" Grounding: Marketing language became theoretical contribution when grounded in Extended Mind thesis
  • Never deliver without full team review: One-agent papers get rejected
